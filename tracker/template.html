    What 1989–92 teaches, and where today differs

    Lessons from 1989–92

    • Rate cuts came first, the bottom came later. The RBA cut from January 1990. Nominal prices still slid for about 15 months and real prices for 30.
    • Unemployment drove the depth. It rose from 5.9% to 11%. Forced sales, not rates alone, kept prices down.
    • No quick boom. Nominal prices took about 4 years to regain the 1989 peak. The next real boom started in 1996–97.
    • Inflation hid the fall. Nominal −4%, real about −12% to −15%.

    What is different now

    • Debt is about 3× larger. Household debt is roughly 185% of disposable income versus about 65% in 1989, so a 4.6% cash rate bites about as hard as 17.5% did.
    • The RBA is still hiking. In mid-1990 it had already cut 2.5 points. Today it has lifted the cash rate 1 point in 2026, with inflation at 4.3%.
    • Prices are falling faster in nominal terms. About −6% in 7 months, Cotality's fastest pace in 40 years.
    • Jobs are softer, not collapsing. Unemployment is at 4.7%, a 5-year high but far from 1990's surge.
    • Supply is tight. Underbuilding and population growth should put a floor under prices sooner than in 1991.
    Method and data

    Six indicators are each scored from −1 to +1 and weighted: RBA direction 25%, home value momentum 25%, unemployment momentum 20%, real cash rate 10%, inflation versus target 10%, GDP growth 10%. Real prices deflate the nominal index by CPI. Scenario paths and priors are analyst judgement; every 10 points of composite below −30 moves 3 percentage points of probability from the early-pivot path to the recession path. Re-run python3 tracker/render.py after adding a row to data/current_2024_2026.csv.

    1988–94 figures are approximate quarterly values reconstructed from RBA, ABS and REIA historical series; treat them as illustrative. 2026 figures are from Cotality's Home Value Index (Jul–Oct 2026), ABS Labour Force (Sep 2026), ABS monthly CPI (Sep 2026) and ABS National Accounts (Jun qtr 2026). Apr–Jun 2026 monthly values are interpolated to match Cotality's −5.2% from-peak reading for September.

    This is a scenario tool, not financial advice or a prediction of any particular property's value.
